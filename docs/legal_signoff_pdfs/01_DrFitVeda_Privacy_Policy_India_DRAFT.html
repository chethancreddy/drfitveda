3. Consent Framework under DPDP Act 2023 and SPDI Rules 2011

In accordance with Section 6 of the DPDP Act 2023, consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action:

  • Granular Affirmative Opt-In: The platform maintains distinct, non-pre-ticked opt-in checkboxes for: (a) Health document upload & sensitive health data processing; (b) Live session recording; and (c) Consultation inquiry callbacks.
  • Unbundled Terms: Acceptance of general commercial Terms & Conditions is never conflated with consent for medical data processing.
  • Right of Withdrawal: Clients have the statutory right to withdraw consent at any time via the client dashboard or by emailing our Grievance Officer. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing conducted prior to withdrawal. Where withdrawal of consent renders it impossible to deliver health or training plans safely, services requiring such data may be terminated.

6. Technical and Organisational Security Measures

In compliance with Section 8(5) of the DPDP Act and Rule 8 of the IT SPDI Rules (Reasonable Security Practices):

  • Encryption: All digital communications are encrypted in transit using TLS 1.3. Stored diagnostic documents and recordings reside in encrypted private storage buckets.
  • Access Isolation: Multi-tenant database separation enforced via PostgreSQL Row Level Security (RLS), ensuring no client can access another client's health records.
  • Temporary Media Links: Media recordings and clinical files are accessed exclusively via cryptographically signed, short-lived URLs expiring within minutes.
  • Security Incident Response: In the event of a personal data breach, Dr Fit Veda will notify the Data Protection Board of India and affected individuals in accordance with Section 8(6) of the DPDP Act.

7. Minors and Eligibility (Age of Majority)

Pursuant to Section 9 of the DPDP Act and the Indian Contract Act, 1872, platform services are restricted to individuals aged 18 years and above. Dr Fit Veda does not knowingly collect personal data from minors or conduct behavioural tracking of children. Accounts identified as belonging to an unauthorised minor will be terminated immediately and associated records expunged.

8. Statutory Data Principal Rights (DPDP Act, 2023)

Registered clients ("Data Principals") enjoy the following enforceable rights under Chapter III of the DPDP Act:

  • Right to Summary & Identity of Data Fiduciaries (Section 11): Request a summary of personal data processed and identities of all entities with whom data has been shared.
  • Right to Correction & Erasure (Section 12): Request correction of inaccurate data, completion of incomplete records, or erasure of personal data that is no longer necessary for the original purpose.
  • Right of Grievance Redressal (Section 13): Right to have grievances redressed by the Data Fiduciary within the prescribed timeline before escalating to the Data Protection Board.
  • Right to Nominate (Section 14): Right to nominate another individual to exercise data rights in the event of death or incapacity.

How to Exercise: Data Principals may exercise these rights via the dedicated Data Subject Request portal at drfitveda.com/data-request or by writing to care@drfitveda.com.
